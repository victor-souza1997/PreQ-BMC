# 1. Executive assessment

The defensible contribution is a case study of local robustness certificates for a fixed-point C neural network: exact-integer CROWN proposes bounds, ESBMC checks generated obligations, and a trusted Python layer binds and composes them. The original C also runs on a VIM3 with recorded accuracy, latency, memory and finite-input parity. The frozen primary evaluation certifies 1/80 test regions; a disclosed rerun certifies 2/80. These are useful implementation and scalability findings, conditional on the stated trust boundary.

The largest acceptance risk is a mismatch between the apparent strength of the claim and the actual proof boundary. “Direct verification of the deployed C,” “all safety obligations,” JNI integration, and a paired-core study overstate the completed work. Low certification coverage and high checking cost also limit a general scalability claim. An artifact-backed experience/case-study framing is better supported than a broad new verification or quantization-synthesis claim. Acceptance remains uncertain: the paper needs a clear incremental contribution and a credible trust argument; the available evidence does not support a numerical acceptance probability.

# 3. Nomenclature audit

Q7.8 is correct only after defining the sign convention. [src/backends/fixed_point.py:24](/home/joao/code/preqbmc/src/backends/fixed_point.py:24) explicitly excludes the sign bit from integer_bits, with total_bits = 1 + integer_bits + fractional_bits. Use:

`<Q,I,F>`, where Q is the total signed width, I excludes the sign bit, and F is the number of fractional bits. Integer n represents n / 2^F.
